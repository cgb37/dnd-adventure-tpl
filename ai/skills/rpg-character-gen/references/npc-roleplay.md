# NPC Roleplay & Personality Reference

Use this reference when generating narrative NPCs with personality, motivations, and social hooks.

## NPC Personality Framework

Every roleplay NPC should have these core elements:

### 1. Motivation (What do they want?)
Pick a primary and secondary motivation:

| Category | Examples |
|----------|---------|
| Survival | Food, shelter, safety, escaping danger |
| Wealth | Gold, land, business, treasure |
| Power | Political influence, military command, magical mastery |
| Knowledge | Secrets, lore, forbidden magic, history |
| Relationships | Love, family, friendship, loyalty, revenge |
| Ideology | Faith, justice, freedom, order, chaos |
| Legacy | Fame, reputation, leaving a mark |
| Redemption | Atoning for past sins, proving worth |

### 2. Personality Traits
Choose 2-3 from different categories:

**Temperament**: Cheerful, melancholic, hot-tempered, stoic, anxious, calm, manic, suspicious
**Social style**: Talkative, reserved, boastful, humble, flirtatious, formal, blunt, evasive
**Quirks**: Collects something odd, speaks in third person, always eating, hums constantly, uses big words incorrectly, quotes proverbs, talks to an imaginary friend

### 3. Physical Distinguishing Feature
One memorable physical detail players will latch onto:
- Unusual scar or mark (burn across one eye, brand on forearm)
- Distinctive voice (raspy whisper, booming laugh, slight stutter)
- Habitual gesture (cracks knuckles, strokes beard, fidgets with coin)
- Clothing signature (always wears red, mismatched boots, too many rings)
- Physical oddity (extremely tall/short, missing finger, heterochromia)

### 4. Secret or Complication
Something beneath the surface that creates depth and potential plot hooks:
- Hidden allegiance (spy, cultist, double agent)
- Shameful past (former criminal, disgraced noble, oath-breaker)
- Forbidden knowledge (knows location of artifact, witnessed a crime)
- Conflicted loyalty (torn between duty and family)
- Cursed or transformed (werewolf, fey-touched, slowly petrifying)

### 5. Relationship to the Party
How might they connect to the adventurers?
- **Quest giver**: Has a task, can pay or reward
- **Ally**: Shares a goal, can help or join temporarily
- **Obstacle**: Blocks progress (gatekeeper, rival, bureaucrat)
- **Information source**: Knows something the party needs
- **Comic relief**: Lightens the mood, memorable but low-stakes
- **Moral challenge**: Forces ethical decisions

## Speech Pattern Guidelines

Give each important NPC a distinct speaking style:

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Formal/archaic | "I beseech thee, consider mine offer ere the morrow." |
| Blunt/short | "No. Not happening. Pay up or leave." |
| Nervous/rambling | "Oh, well, you see, the thing is—and I don't mean to trouble you—but perhaps..." |
| Scholarly | "Fascinating. The parallels to the Third Dynasty's collapse are quite striking, actually." |
| Street/slang | "Look mate, I ain't got all day. You want the goods or what?" |
| Cryptic/poetic | "The river knows the stone's secret, but only the moon can hear it sing." |

## NPC Complexity Tiers

Match NPC depth to narrative importance:

### Tier 1: Background NPCs
- Name + occupation + 1 trait
- Example: "Marta, baker, always dusted with flour"
- No stat block needed

### Tier 2: Recurring NPCs
- Name + occupation + 2-3 traits + motivation + speech pattern
- Example: Full tavern keeper with personality
- Optional simple stat block (commoner/guard base)

### Tier 3: Major NPCs
- Full personality framework (all 5 elements above)
- Detailed backstory and relationships
- Full stat block with customization
- Multiple plot hooks tied to their secrets/goals
